### Assessment

The build covers the core of the supervisor problem better than the alert products users have today.
It is strongest on P5 and P11, and has a solid base for P1, P2 and P4.

The gaps that matter most, ranked by how strongly the research supports them:

1. **P3, local entity resolution.** Supervisors judge relevance by the SG entity, and the model has no subsidiary or branch level. This affects every entity feature.
2. **P4, adverse and sentiment in the UI.** The data already exists (`adverse`, direction); it isn't surfaced or filterable. Smallest effort for a directly requested need.
3. **P1, "why this matters" per development.** The answers carry implications at subject level, but users scan item by item.
4. **P7, management brief export.** Three users consolidate for bosses by hand; nothing in the build helps.
5. **P2, adding entities without editing YAML.** Needed before supervisors can use it for their 20+ entities.
6. **P12, push alerts and a scheduler.** In-app alerts only help a user who opens the app; users said they read on the go.

P14 (internal exposure data) and social media are real needs but depend on data access outside this POC.

Most interviewed departments work on micro data: named institutions and their conduct.
RMD is macro; RiMD is mixed; FDD and FTIG look at financial centres and sectors.

MAS supervises institutions under its Framework for Impact and Risk Assessment (CRAFT), which rates each by impact (systemic importance) and risk.
The "impact x imminence" view users asked for matches that framing.

### Personas

1. **Prudential supervisor** (BD1, BD2, ID, partly PD).
   Watches named institutions and needs adverse news, local entity resolution, peers and exposure.
   MAS rates the firms it supervises on impact and risk under a published framework (CRAFT), the same idea as the "impact x imminence" view users asked for, so the feature can speak supervisors' own language.
   Pain points P2 to P6, P12, P14.
2. **Financial crime supervisor** (AMLD, partly PD).
   Cares about specific adverse event types (ML, sanctions, scams, hacks) and patterns across a sector more than about single entities.
   Pain points P4, P5, P12.
3. **Markets and investment** (RMD, partly RiMD).
   Cares about markets, macro and analyst views.
   Market sentiment and direction are useful to this group, and the one-paragraph summary feature fits them best.
   The EPG econtech dashboards (below) already cover much of their macro data, so IntelMonitor serves them through news, analyst views and summaries rather than series.
   Pain points P1, P8, P13.
4. **Development and policy** (FDD, FTIG).
   Positive news matters here too: relocations, new products, rival financial centres.
   A filter that only shows adverse news would fail them.
   Pain points P8, P13.
5. **Curator** (EKD).
   Produces newsletters for everyone else, so needs briefs and export.
   Pain points P1, P7, P11.

### The EPG econtech dashboards (mas-epg-econtech.github.io)

Surveyed on 5 Oct 2026.
Six dashboards are linked from the landing page, and two more (Middle East Monitor, Energy Dashboard) are in the GitHub org.
One economist built them in about a month with Claude for an internal MAS AI showcase in May 2026.

| Dashboard | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Singapore Price Tracker | Daily CPI proxy from about 79k supermarket product prices; Claude assigns CPI categories |
| Global Shipping Nowcast | Chokepoint and port traffic against a no-crisis counterfactual |
| Iran Crisis Trade Monitor | Monthly Gulf trade exposure; a one-off snapshot |
| SGX Sentiment Index | Inflation, uncertainty, business and labour sentiment from about 60k SGX filings, tested as leading indicators |
| Singapore Property Market | 56k PropertyGuru listings; price cuts as a leading indicator |
| ASEAN Markets | FX, 10-year yields, commodities |
| Middle East Monitor | How the Iran/Hormuz shock reaches Singapore and Asia; about 130 CEIC and Bloomberg series; AI status per pillar |
| Energy Dashboard | Energy supply-chain transmission map; superseded |

The suite is macro-focused: firm-level inputs are aggregated into macro indices checked against CPI, GDP and unemployment.
It has no news feed, entity tracking, institution-level data or alerting, so it does not overlap with IntelMonitor's supervisor personas.
Two parts are worth reusing:

- The Middle East Monitor's status per pillar (Calm, Watchful, Strained, Critical) with key drivers citing charts and a list of data gaps, regenerated only on a 2σ move or after 7 days.
  It is a better answer to the "general sentiment" request than a percentage.
- The SGX filings pipeline (2,223 issuers), which could feed per-company signals to supervisors.

### Feedback after the research

- **Peers and exposure (potential user, Oct 2026).**
  A supervisor's page for a bank should show news on its peer banks and on markets and sectors the bank is exposed to.
  Example: adverse news in the Indian property market should reach UOB's supervisor through UOB's exposure to India, even when the article never names UOB.
  This supports P5, P6 and P14.
  The phase A eval confirmed the gap: on a UOB exposure question every variant found 0 of 3 relevant developments, because retrieval is scoped to documents about UOB itself.
- **One-paragraph summary and general sentiment (users, Oct 2026).**
  Users want all news and analysis summarised in one paragraph, with a general sentiment such as "80% positive" or "40% risk".
  Recommended form: the paragraph plus a direction (more or less adverse than last week) and counts of developments, not articles.
  A share of articles measures coverage, not risk, and one user said the direction matters more than the number.

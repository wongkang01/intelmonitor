UOBvia its Singapore loans, 49.8% of totalMixed

Nearly half of UOB's loans are in Singapore, and a Sora rise lifts loan yields and margins, which the Business Times says UOB is most likely to feel. Higher rates also raise the risk of stress among borrowers.

UOB maintained full-year NIM guidance of 1.75 to 1.80 per cent at its second-quarter results. Higher rates, however, also raise the risk of stress among borrowers.

Public: UOB half-year financial statements, p. 12, S$179,903m of loans·Figures at 30 Jun 2026·Checked 7 Oct 2026

DBSvia its Singapore loans, 44.4% of totalMixed

With 44.4% of loans in Singapore, a higher Sora should help DBS income, and Morningstar still sees it as a net beneficiary of higher rates. The gain is partly offset by its US dollar book, which is negatively sensitive to higher rates, and by borrower stress risk.

For DBS, the impact is less straightforward. The lender said in August that its US dollar book remained negatively sensitive to higher rates, with annualised NII falling about S$4 million for every one basis point rise in US dollar rates.

Public: DBS 2Q26 performance summary, p. 20, S$210,852m of loans·Figures at 30 Jun 2026·Checked 7 Oct 2026

OCBCvia its Singapore loans, 43.5% of totalMixed

OCBC has 43.5% of loans in Singapore, so a higher Sora should lift loan pricing and margins after a quarter of slightly lower NII. The passages give a smaller share of Singapore dollar loans for OCBC than for UOB, and higher rates can raise borrower stress, so the net effect is modest.

OCBC’s NII slipped 1 per cent to S$2.26 billion, while UOB’s fell 2 per cent to S$2.3 billion. Their quarterly net profits nevertheless rose 9 per cent to S$3.08 billion, 22 per cent to S$2.22 billion and 10 per cent to S$1.48 billion, respectively.

Public: OCBC 1H2026 media release, p. 14, S$158,688m of loans·Figures at 30 Jun 2026·Checked 7 Oct 2026
